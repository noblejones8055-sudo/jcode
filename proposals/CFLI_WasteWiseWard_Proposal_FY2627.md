## Setting the Scene

### A Morning in Ward 97, Harrawala

*Before most of Dehradun stirs, the Ward 97 team is already moving.*

*Green Workers pull on gloves, load collection vehicles, and begin making their rounds through Harrawala's lanes. Wet waste here, dry waste there — the rhythm of the morning as familiar as the faces at each door. Some residents hand over neatly sorted bins; others still need a nudge. The team gives it, patiently, as they do every single day.*

*Behind this quiet, essential work is a reality that rarely makes headlines. These workers breathe the same air that carries what they collect. Their lungs absorb the risks that the city does not see. Their bodies carry the weight of a system that depends on them, yet rarely asks how they are.*

*At the same time, something remarkable has happened in this ward. Neighbours who once tossed mixed waste into the lane now separate their kitchen scraps from their plastics. Shopkeepers who once ignored the bin outside now know which colour sack is for which waste. Children who once watched waste pile up now take it home instead of leaving it on the road. This did not happen by accident — it happened because a system showed up, consistently, and earned the community's trust.*

*This proposal is about sustaining and deepening that system. It is about the 2,000 households whose environment Waste Warriors helps protect. It is about the workers whose health and dignity deserve the same attention as the waste they manage. And it is about the quiet but powerful connection between a cleaner neighbourhood and a healthier one — a connection that CFLI's support can help us prove at scale.*

## About Waste Warriors

Waste Warriors Society is a non-profit organisation catalysing systemic change to solve the waste management crisis of the Indian Himalayan Region. Our work lies at the intersection of **Climate Change, Biodiversity Habitat Conservation, and Informal Livelihoods** — through bringing behavioural change in communities and building the infrastructure that makes change stick.

With a team of **240+ Warriors** spread across Uttarakhand and Himachal Pradesh, we focus on enabling better governance, addressing infrastructure and policy gaps, and activating communities to co-create lasting solutions. We promote local entrepreneurship and ignite a sense of urgency among women and youth to earn their identity through dignified, meaningful work.

Our project locations span **12 sites** across both states — from Shimla, Manali, Kasauli, Dharamshala, and Bir-Billing in Himachal Pradesh, to Kempty (Mussoorie), Dehradun City, Sahastradhara, Govind Wildlife Sanctuary (Uttarkashi), and Corbett Tiger Reserve (Ramnagar) in Uttarakhand.

## The Challenge: Dehradun's Waste & Wellness Crisis

Dehradun — Uttarakhand's capital — is home to approximately 1.7 million residents and growing. It is a city of institutions, of education, of natural beauty. It is also a city under serious and worsening environmental pressure.

Across its 100 municipal wards, Dehradun generates hundreds of metric tons of solid waste every single day. Only a small fraction of this is treated scientifically. The rest is dumped in open spaces, burned on roadsides, or sent to overflowing, unscientific landfills. The air, the soil, the waterways — each bears the consequence.

But the consequences are not shared equally.

**For residents of lower-income and peri-urban neighbourhoods**, a broken waste system means living next to open dumps, breathing air thickened with burning plastic, and watching monsoon rains convert plastic-choked drains into floodwater that enters homes and businesses.

**For informal waste workers**, it means handling hazardous materials without protective gear, earning irregular incomes with no job security, and living with occupational health risks that accumulate silently over years of physical labour.

**For the city's ecology**, it means contaminated groundwater, degraded soil, and a creeping toxic load in streams that feed into the Himalayan watershed — waterways on which billions of lives, far beyond Dehradun, ultimately depend.

**The connection between waste and wellness is direct:**

- Open burning of municipal waste releases black carbon and fine particulate matter — primary contributors to respiratory disease among children, the elderly, and frontline workers.
- Poorly managed wet waste breeds vectors for dengue, typhoid, and cholera — diseases that disproportionately affect communities already stretched thin.
- Informal waste workers who lack medical coverage, nutritional support, and occupational safety protections carry health vulnerabilities that compound over time.
- Neighbourhoods trapped in cycles of waste accumulation lose civic confidence — the belief that collective action can produce collective change.

Waste management in Dehradun, then, is not simply a sanitation question. It is a **public wellness challenge.** And it requires an intervention that treats it as such — addressing not just the waste, but the people embedded in the system that manages it.

## Ward 97: A Government-Endorsed Proof of Concept

### How It Started

In 2020, something unusual happened. The **Municipal Corporation of Dehradun (Nagar Nigam)** formally entrusted Ward 97, Harrawala, to Waste Warriors Society — with a mandate to develop it into Dehradun's model ward for decentralised, scientific solid waste management. Alongside this, the government **provided the land at Harrawala** for the establishment of a Material Recovery Facility (MRF).

This was not a small gesture. It was an institutional bet — a recognition by the city's civic body that community-based, professionally managed waste systems could outperform the status quo. Nagar Nigam gave us the mandate. The Forest Research Institute (FRI) supported the facility's design. The Lal Bahadur Shastri National Academy of Administration (LBSNAA) began sending trainees to observe the model. Over time, **Ward 97 became a reference point** — the place decision-makers visit when they want to understand what urban waste management looks like when it works.

### What Has Been Built

Over five years of operations, the Ward 97 programme has developed into a **full-stack, decentralised waste management system** serving over 2,000 households, commercial establishments, and residential welfare associations across Harrawala. It brings together:

**Door-to-door segregated collection** of dry, wet, and sanitary waste — a twice-daily service that reaches every corner of the ward, managed by a trained team of Green Workers (formally employed frontline staff) who accompany the collection vehicle and actively support households in maintaining segregation at source.

**The Harrawala Material Recovery Facility (MRF)** — Uttarakhand's first and largest facility of its kind — where all collected waste undergoes secondary segregation into 30+ material categories. Recyclables are baled and sent to verified downstream recyclers. Wet waste enters a composting stream. Reject waste is routed to co-processing rather than open dumping. Hazardous and sanitary streams are managed separately, preventing contamination.

**A sustained behaviour change programme** that has moved Ward 97 from near-zero source segregation to a rate that is among the highest of any ward in Dehradun — achieved through consistent community engagement, peer advocacy, and the steady presence of a team that shows up every day.

**A growing community of civic champions** — residents who operate as Swachhta Ambassadors within their zones, commercial establishments that segregate routinely, and institutions that treat the ward's cleanliness as a shared responsibility rather than someone else's job.

The Harrawala MRF today receives visits from Indian Forest Service officers, urban development officials from across Uttarakhand, LBSNAA trainees, and civil society partners — not as a showcase, but as a working demonstration of a model ready to be replicated.

**The government gave us the land and the mandate. The community gave us their trust. CFLI's support will help us make the most of both.**

## The Project: Waste Wise Ward

### Our Focus

The Waste Wise Ward programme deepens and expands the impact of Waste Warriors' ongoing operations in Ward 97, Harrawala — with a specific, deliberate focus on **community wellness** and **Green Worker health and dignity.**

This is a six-month intervention. It does not build a new system from the ground up — it strengthens a system that a municipal government has already endorsed, a community has already invested trust in, and five years of consistent operations have already proven. What CFLI's support enables is the layer of the work that is too often left underfunded: the wellness dimension.

We are asking CFLI to invest in three interconnected dimensions of wellness:

- **Community wellness** — through cleaner environments, sustained behaviour change, and reduced exposure to the health hazards of poorly managed waste
- **Worker wellness** — through structured health, nutrition, and livelihood support for the Green Workers whose daily labour makes this system possible
- **Civic wellness** — through youth engagement, government partnership, and the dissemination of a working model that Dehradun's other wards can learn from and adopt

### The Processing Backbone

At the heart of the Ward 97 system is the Harrawala MRF — where the waste collected every day undergoes a rigorous processing journey. Dry waste is sorted into 30+ material categories and baled for downstream recyclers. Multi-layered plastics — the hardest-to-manage waste stream — are handled through specialised processing that converts a cost burden into a revenue stream. Wet waste is composted, turning organic refuse into nutrient-rich material that reduces landfill dependency and creates a product with real agricultural value.

Sanitary waste — often the most overlooked stream in urban waste systems — is segregated and diverted from landfills through dedicated processing, preventing soil and groundwater contamination.

The MRF is not just a processing facility. It is a **livelihood hub** for the Green Workers who operate it. It is a **learning centre** for the city officials and trainees who visit it. It is the **anchor of the entire circular waste chain** in Ward 97. Without the MRF, collection has nowhere to go. Without the collection system, the MRF has no feedstock. The two are fully interdependent — and this proposal funds both.

## Our Activities

The Waste Wise Ward programme is organised around **five thematic pillars**, each addressing a distinct dimension of community and worker wellness.

---

### Pillar 1: Clean Environments, Healthy Lives
**Theme: Behaviour Change & Community IEC**

The foundation of a clean, healthy neighbourhood is a community that understands why waste management matters — and has the tools, the knowledge, and the peer support to act on that understanding.

**Chai pe Charcha** — Waste Warriors' signature neighbourhood dialogue programme — brings residents together over tea to surface local waste concerns, share simple solutions, and build the kind of everyday accountability that outlasts any campaign. These sessions are not lectures; they are conversations between neighbours. And they are one of the most effective behaviour change tools we have. Each session focuses on a specific theme: source segregation, wet waste management, safe disposal of sanitary waste, or responsible consumption during festivals and events.

**Cleanup Drives** serve a dual purpose: they remove accumulated waste from public spaces and streets, while creating a platform for youth to step forward as civic actors. Every cleanup is also a community conversation — an opportunity to talk, in an accessible, action-oriented context, about why waste should not be here in the first place. Youth who participate in cleanups are our most effective word-of-mouth ambassadors for segregation at home.

**Visual Communication** — signboards, murals, and IEC material at high-footfall points across Ward 97 — ensure that the message of responsible waste management is visible even when the team is not physically present. These installations create a permanent visual grammar of accountability in the neighbourhood.

**Wet Waste Awareness** is a specific programme thread: helping households understand that kitchen and garden waste, managed correctly, need not end up in a plastic bag at the street corner. Through door-to-door engagement and community sessions, we promote home composting as a wellness practice — good for the household, good for the neighbourhood, good for the soil.

---

### Pillar 2: Waste to Resource — The Processing Chain
**Theme: MRF Operations & Circular Economy**

Every piece of waste that leaves a household in Ward 97 is destined for the Harrawala MRF — where the real work of resource recovery happens. Green Workers perform the skilled, labour-intensive task of secondary segregation, sorting materials into 30+ categories before they move downstream to verified recyclers, co-processors, and composters.

The processing chain is where environmental impact is made concrete. Recyclables that reach a verified recycler do not reach a landfill. Organic matter that enters a composting system does not rot in the open, releasing methane. Multi-layered plastics that find a co-processing route do not burn on a roadside. Each decision made at the MRF has a downstream consequence — environmental and economic.

The MRF also generates revenue through the sale of recovered materials, which is reinvested into operational continuity and Green Worker livelihoods. This revenue — modest but real — is part of the gradual progress toward a financially self-sustaining model that does not depend indefinitely on grant funding.

Vehicle operations — fuel, maintenance, and logistics — sustain the collection-to-processing chain without interruption through the six months of the programme.

---

### Pillar 3: Dignity at Work — Green Worker Wellness
**Theme: Occupational Health, Nutrition & Worker Support**

A waste management system is only as strong as the people who run it. Yet in most of India's cities, the workers who sort and process waste daily are among the least protected, least recognised, and most medically vulnerable members of the urban workforce. Their bodies carry risks that accumulate silently over years of physical labour.

Waste Warriors has consistently refused to treat worker welfare as an afterthought. In Ward 97, all Green Workers are formally employed with fair wages, safe conditions, and a structured wellness programme that addresses occupational and personal wellbeing comprehensively. CFLI's support will sustain and strengthen every element of this commitment.

**Annual Medical Screening:** All Green Workers and team members receive an annual health check-up covering routine bloodwork and vitamin screenings. For workers whose hands, lungs, and bodies absorb the daily risks of waste handling, early detection is not a luxury — it is an occupational obligation.

**Wellness & Medical Allowances:** A structured allowance covers ad-hoc medical expenses, treatments, and health-related needs that arise outside the annual screening. An additional **Hobby Fund** — recognising that wellbeing does not end at the MRF gate — supports Green Workers and team members in pursuing personal interests and recreational activities, for balance and mental wellness beyond the workplace.

**Meal Programme:** Daily nutritional support ensures that workers performing strenuous, outdoor physical labour are adequately nourished throughout their shift. This is a direct wellness investment, not a perk.

**Insurance & Gratuity:** Formal employment protections — insurance coverage and gratuity provisions — give Green Workers the baseline security and legal standing that workers in more visible sectors take for granted.

**Plastic Lao, Rashan Pao — A Past Innovation Worth Noting:**
As an example of the creative, welfare-oriented thinking that runs through Waste Warriors' approach, the organisation previously ran the *Plastic Lao, Rashan Pao* initiative — where informal waste pickers who brought in low-value plastic that is typically uneconomical to collect received ration provisions in return. The initiative addressed two problems at once: it created an economic incentive for diverting a difficult-to-manage waste stream, and it provided direct nutritional support to some of Dehradun's most economically precarious workers. It is the kind of intervention that demonstrates how waste infrastructure can also function as a social safety net — and it reflects how we think about the communities our work touches.

---

### Pillar 4: Growing People — Vocational Training & Capacity Building
**Theme: Skills, Literacy & Learning**

A frontline waste worker's skill set is, of necessity, practical. But the barriers to economic mobility do not end at the MRF gate. For many Green Workers, language and digital literacy are the invisible walls that keep economic opportunity out of reach.

**Weekly Spoken English Sessions** for Green Workers build communication confidence and practical language skills. These are not English fluency courses. They are dignity investments — sessions that help workers engage more effectively with the government officials, LBSNAA trainees, IFS officers, and civil society visitors who regularly come to the Harrawala MRF. A Green Worker who can explain what they do, in English, to an IAS officer, is a worker whose dignity is visible. These sessions open pathways to better-paying employment, improved self-advocacy, and a broader sense of economic possibility.

**Digital Literacy** is equally important as Waste Warriors deepens its internal digital infrastructure — from procurement and inventory management systems to WhatsApp-based community communication tools and real-time waste issue reporting applications. Ensuring that frontline team members can navigate these tools confidently is both an operational necessity and an equity commitment.

**Cross-Learning Exposure Visit:** The programme includes a structured learning exchange — an opportunity for project staff to visit a high-performing waste management model at another site, observe different approaches, bring insights back to Ward 97, and apply them. In waste management, as in most fields, the most powerful teacher is a system that works somewhere else, seen with your own eyes. This visit is an investment in institutional learning and the continuous improvement of the Ward 97 model.

---

### Pillar 5: Systemic Change — Government Partnership & Dissemination
**Theme: Civic Engagement, Policy Influence & Model Replication**

The Ward 97 model was designed, from the beginning, to be borrowed. Its documentation, its processes, and its results have always been oriented toward one goal beyond Harrawala itself: giving Dehradun's other wards — and urban local bodies across Uttarakhand — a working template that can be adapted and adopted.

**Municipal Engagement & Enforcement:** Active, ongoing collaboration with Dehradun Nagar Nigam ensures that the Ward 97 model has institutional backing beyond Waste Warriors' own capacity. This includes capacity-building workshops for ward-level officials, joint segregation awareness drives, enforcement support for segregation norms, and sustained dialogue with municipal decision-makers about the pathway to adopting this model in additional wards. The goal is not to run Ward 97 indefinitely — it is to hand over an increasingly self-sufficient model to the city that commissioned it.

**Dissemination & Networking Event at the MRF — A Landmark Moment:**
As the programme matures, Waste Warriors will host a **signature dissemination and networking event at the Harrawala MRF itself** — bringing together municipal officials, government representatives, institutional partners, members of the donor community, and civic leaders in the same space where the work happens every day.

This is not a conference room presentation with a slide deck. It is a **live demonstration** — guests walk the facility, see the processing lines, meet the Green Workers who run them, and leave with a first-hand understanding of what decentralised waste management looks like in operation. The event will include panel discussions on urban SWM policy, networking between government and civil society actors, and a recognition moment for the Green Workers and community members whose sustained effort has made Ward 97 what it is.

This event will coincide with **Waste Warriors' Foundation Day** — marking 14 years of work in the Indian Himalayan Region. It is a moment to reflect on the journey, celebrate the people who built it, and publicly commit to the next phase — with the CFLI partnership as a milestone in that story.
